# Website Review for Dr. Varadharajan V — What the New NMC Rules Mean for Your Site

**Prepared by:** Automize Media Labs · **Date:** 26 October 2026 · **Site reviewed:** drvaradharajan.com

## What has changed

The National Medical Commission has issued new guidelines on how doctors and hospitals may present themselves publicly, including on websites. The basic principle is simple: a doctor's website may **inform**, but it may not **promote**. A few common website features that used to be routine — patient testimonials, surgery counts, "why choose us" messaging — are now specifically not allowed.

The good news: most of your website is already in good shape. The educational articles, the way your qualifications and Tamil Nadu Medical Council registration number are displayed, the honest way costs are handled, and the absence of discount offers or gimmicks are all exactly what the new rules want.

## What we found that needs to change

1. **Patient testimonials ("What Our Patients Say")** — the three named patient quotes on the homepage are not permitted, even if the patients are real and willing. We will remove this section.

2. **Surgery and patient numbers** — "5,000+ breast surgeries", "3,500+ minimal access surgeries" and "25,000+ lives touched" appear on the homepage, the About and Specialities pages, four service pages and the site's machine-readable summary. Stating numbers of surgeries or patients is now prohibited. We will replace them with things that are allowed and verifiable: your qualifications, fellowships, memberships, and years in practice.

3. **"Built & Marketed by Automize Media Labs" footer credit** — the word "Marketed" publicly states that a third party markets a doctor's services, which the rules do not allow. We will remove or neutralise it (this is our change to make, not yours).

4. **Where your photo is used** — the rules allow one plain photo on your profile page. Right now your portrait also appears in the homepage banner next to the booking button and statistics card, on the social-media share card, in the "Ready to take the next step" banner, and inside every treatment-page image. We will keep the photo only where identification is the point, and remove it from the promotional spots.

5. **Computer-generated illustrations** — the artwork on blog articles and treatment pages is AI-generated but nowhere labelled as such. We will add a short note to your editorial policy and label these images; a few that show a surgeon figure will be replaced with anatomy-only artwork.

6. **A few smaller wording and disclosure fixes** — adding a short "risks and limitations" note to each treatment page, retitling "Why Choose" and "Global Recognition" headings in plainer language, correcting the privacy policy to mention the YouTube videos, and adding one line to the appointment form about how patient details are used.

## What we need from you

- **Confirm the testimonials' source** — are the three quotes real patient statements? (Either way they must come down; this just tells us how to describe the removal.)
- **Your years in practice** — a confirmed number to replace the surgery counts, if you'd like one shown.
- **Confirm the images are AI-generated** — so the disclosure wording is accurate.
- **Whose site this is** — yours personally, or Iswarya Hospital's page for you? It affects how we word the branding.
- **A quick check of your other profiles** — your Google Business Profile, Facebook, LinkedIn and Instagram (@answers.to.cancer) should follow the same rules: no testimonial reposts, no offers, no "best doctor" wording. We've listed exactly what to check in the full report.

## What stays the same

Your articles, treatment guides, FAQs, contact and booking options, WhatsApp video consultations, photo of the hospital, conference updates and educational videos all stay — they are what the guidelines encourage. Nothing about how patients find or contact you will change.
